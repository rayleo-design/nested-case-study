01

About the project

Nested is a mobile CRM for real-estate Relationship Managers, who take every property enquiry from first call to booking. The app is the only tool they carry all day.

As the sole designer, I owned the product end to end. The brief: redesign the RM experience and build in intelligence, so the app guides the day instead of just recording it. This case study follows the four features that moved RMs off Excel and onto the app.

Nested is a stand-in name. The work is real; the client is under NDA, so every screen here was rebuilt from scratch.

02

The challenge

RMs didn't need more features. They needed answers to the same four questions, every morning, on a phone, between calls.

Before designing anything, I sat with RMs and watched them work. Their day ran across eight tools that didn't talk to each other, so every answer meant digging: incentive math in Excel, priorities on gut feel, lead history buried in WhatsApp. The app had to answer each question fast, on mobile, without making the RM dig.

  1. 01

    “How am I doing today?”

    No single view of the day. Targets, visits and follow-ups sat in different tools, so every morning started with digging.

  2. 02

    “How much will I earn?”

    Nobody knew until month-end. Incentive math lived in Excel, and RMs didn't trust the numbers until the paycheck arrived.

  3. 03

    “Who do I call first?”

    Based on gut feeling. No scoring, no prioritisation, nothing telling them where to focus.

  4. 04

    “What happened with this lead?”

    RMs scrolled back through WhatsApp, and there was no shared context when more than one RM spoke to the same lead.

Each question became a chapter of the redesign. Thumb-first and one question per screen became the ground rules for all four.

03

The UX work

The structure went through two real shapes before it answered the RM's four questions.

I shadowed five RMs, timed every switch between apps, and turned their day into an information architecture. The first version failed in testing, in a useful way. The second one became Nested.

  1. Shadowed RMs through full shifts

    Five RMs, two offices. Every switch between apps got a timestamp.

    The tools weren't the problem. The missing structure between them was.

  2. Grouped the app by data source

    Leads from Sheets, chats from WhatsApp, incentives from Excel, visits from Calendar. One app, same four silos.

    One lead ended up in three branches.

  3. Tested it with RMs, mid-shift

    A clickable prototype, real leads on the line. I watched where thumbs went and what got asked out loud.

    Tabs broke live calls. Incentives were the first thing anyone looked for.

  4. Regrouped by the question being asked

    Each section answers one of the four questions from the challenge. A lead lives in one place, and everything else links to it.

    Retest: every RM found their incentive in under ten seconds.

Two maps. One that matched how an RM actually thinks.

06

The AI layer

From signal to suggestion, in five layers.

Every AI moment in Nested follows the same rules, so RMs learn when to trust it and when to check.

  1. The AI reads calls, WhatsApp, email and visits logged in the app. Anything said in person or on a personal phone isn't counted, and the app says so.

  2. Leads get Hot, Warm or Cold from engagement signals, but only after two of them. Until then the lead shows Pending, not a guess.

  3. The Daily Brief pins the three things most likely to slip. Each card is labelled as an AI suggestion, with its confidence.

  4. “Why this?” opens the signals behind every pick, each marked Strong, Medium or Weak, and how many it used.

  5. Summaries and drafts are labelled as AI, drafts are never sent on their own, and the RM can change any score in one tap.
